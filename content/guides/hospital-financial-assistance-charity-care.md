## Start with the hospital's written policy

Hospital financial assistance, sometimes called **charity care**, can reduce or eliminate eligible charges for people who meet a facility's rules. It is an application process, not a guaranteed discount and not one national program with a single income limit.

> **Illustrative example notice:** Every name, household, account reference, date, income change, and dollar amount below is synthetic. No example comes from a real patient or predicts that an application will be approved.

The [medical billing glossary](/glossary) explains terms such as financial assistance policy, amounts generally billed, and patient responsibility. If you first need to organize the charges you were sent, request a detailed statement using [How Do You Get an Itemized Hospital Bill in 2026?](/guides/how-to-get-itemized-hospital-bill). The [Medical Bill Reader analyzer](/#analyzer) can provide an informational explanation of a document you choose to share; review its current privacy, availability, and pricing disclosures before using it.

## What Section 501(r) requires

Internal Revenue Code Section 501(r) applies to tax-exempt hospital organizations described in Section 501(c)(3). The requirements operate facility by facility. They do not automatically cover every for-profit hospital, independent clinic, laboratory, ambulance company, or physician practice.

For a covered hospital facility, the IRS requires a written financial assistance policy, or FAP, that applies at least to emergency and other medically necessary care provided by the hospital and certain substantially related entities. The policy must explain:

- Who is eligible and whether assistance means free or discounted care
- How the hospital calculates the amount charged to an eligible person
- How to apply
- Billing and collection actions, either in the policy or a separate policy
- Whether information from other sources may be used for presumptive eligibility
- Which other providers delivering emergency or medically necessary care in the hospital are covered and which are not

The provider list is important. A hospital may approve assistance for its facility account while an emergency physician, anesthesiology group, radiology group, or other separately billing provider is outside that policy. Ask each separate billing entity whether it has its own assistance program.

## How to find the correct policy and application

Search the official hospital website for “financial assistance,” “charity care,” or “patient financial services.” Confirm that the policy names the facility where you received care and check the policy's effective date.

IRS rules require covered hospitals to make the FAP, application, and plain-language summary widely available online. They also must make paper copies available without charge upon request, by mail and in public hospital locations including the emergency room and admissions areas. Billing statements should include a conspicuous notice about available assistance and contact information.

If you cannot find the documents, use this request:

> “Please send me the current financial assistance policy, plain-language summary, application form, and the list of providers covered by the policy for [facility name]. Please also tell me the application deadline, where to submit it, and who can help with questions. I am requesting these documents without charge.”

Ask whether translated materials or language assistance are available if needed. Section 501(r) includes translation and public-notice requirements for significant limited-English-proficiency populations served by the facility.

## Who qualifies varies by hospital

There is no single federal percentage of income that guarantees charity care at every hospital. One facility may use federal poverty guidelines, household size, assets, insurance status, underinsurance, residency, medical debt relative to income, or a combination. Another may offer more generous assistance than federal tax rules require.

Read the policy rather than relying on a general online calculator. Look for:

- The income period the hospital uses, such as recent monthly income or a tax year
- How the policy defines a household or family
- Whether insured and underinsured patients may apply
- Whether residency or service-area rules apply
- Which types of care are considered medically necessary
- Which providers and accounts are included
- The application deadline and requested documentation
- Any reconsideration or appeal process after a denial

CMS says nonprofit hospitals must provide financial assistance to eligible people who cannot afford to pay. “Eligible” is doing important work in that sentence: the hospital's established policy decides whether the applicant qualifies. Do not treat nonprofit status as automatic forgiveness of every balance.

## What to include in an application

Follow the hospital's instructions exactly. Federal law does not prescribe one universal consumer document packet. A hospital may request information or supporting records described in its FAP or application.

A practical application file may include, when the hospital requests it:

1. The completed and signed hospital application
2. The hospital account or statement reference and relevant dates of care
3. Household size using the policy's definition
4. Current income documentation in the form the policy accepts
5. Insurance information or an EOB when relevant
6. A short factual explanation of a recent job loss, income reduction, or other changed circumstance
7. Any separate documentation the written policy specifically lists

Submit only what is needed. Keep sensitive records out of ordinary email unless the hospital directs you to a verified secure method. Save a copy of the complete packet and proof of submission.

### A concise cover note

> **Subject: Financial assistance application for account [reference]**
>
> I am applying under the current financial assistance policy for [facility]. Enclosed are the completed application and the supporting documents listed in the policy. My household or financial circumstances relevant to the application are summarized as follows: [brief factual explanation].
>
> Please confirm receipt, tell me whether anything is missing, and provide the expected review time. Please also explain in writing how billing and collection activity will be handled while the application is under review.

Do not include a diagnosis or detailed medical history unless the policy genuinely requires information relevant to eligibility. The application is financial, and unnecessary health information increases privacy exposure.

## Apply even if insurance processed the claim

Having insurance does not automatically prevent eligibility. IRS guidance says the Section 501(r) limit on amounts generally billed applies to both insured and uninsured people who qualify under the facility's FAP.

An insured applicant may still have a deductible, coinsurance, noncovered balance, or other patient responsibility. The policy decides which amounts and services it covers. Compare the hospital account with the EOB first so you can identify the actual patient balance. [How Do You Read an EOB Line by Line in 2026?](/guides/how-to-read-an-eob) explains that reconciliation.

Do not assume financial assistance will erase an insurer appeal issue. If the plan denied a claim, preserve the plan's deadline and follow [How Do You Appeal a Health Insurance Denial in 2026?](/guides/appeal-health-insurance-claim-denial) while the hospital evaluates assistance.

## Ask what happens to the bill while the application is pending

CMS recommends checking the status after applying and asking how long review will take, whom to contact, and what happens with the bill in the meantime. If a collector is involved, CMS advises telling the collector that you are seeking financial help and asking it to pause collection while the process plays out.

Ask what happens while the application is pending; do not assume that collection activity automatically stops.

For hospitals subject to Section 501(r), the IRS requires reasonable efforts to determine whether a person is FAP-eligible before the hospital engages in extraordinary collection actions. IRS guidance describes:

- A **120-day notification period** beginning when the first post-discharge billing statement is provided
- A **240-day application period** beginning on that same date
- Notice about how to complete an incomplete application submitted during the application period, plus a reasonable opportunity to do so
- An eligibility determination for a complete application submitted during the application period

The application period can be longer in some circumstances because the hospital must give advance notice before initiating certain extraordinary collection actions. Those federal rules concern covered facilities and extraordinary actions; they should not be rewritten as a promise that all statements, calls, interest, or every third-party activity stops immediately.

Request the answer for your account in writing. Continue tracking dates and do not ignore a summons or other legal notice. Individual legal advice may be appropriate if litigation or wage-related collection is involved.

## What charge limits mean after approval

For emergency or other medically necessary care, Section 501(r)(5) limits what a covered hospital may charge a FAP-eligible individual to no more than the amounts generally billed to people with insurance. For other care covered by the FAP, the person's responsibility must be less than gross charges.

“Amounts generally billed” is a regulatory calculation, not a universal price list or a fixed discount percentage. Hospitals can use permitted calculation methods, and the result can vary by facility. Ask the hospital to show how it applied its policy to your account rather than relying on an online estimate of what the discount should be.

Approval also may require the hospital to correct amounts already collected above the eligible responsibility, subject to regulatory details. Ask for a revised statement showing the original charge, assistance adjustment, payments, and remaining balance.

## A synthetic application example

Assume Jordan receives a **$3,200** hospital-facility balance after insurance. Jordan's income recently fell, but the hospital's summary statement does not explain its assistance rules.

Jordan downloads the facility's current FAP, application, and provider list from the official site. The policy says which income records it accepts and identifies the hospital account as covered, but a separate physician group is not covered. Jordan submits the completed form and only the requested financial documents through the hospital's secure channel, keeps a copy, and asks for confirmation.

Jordan also contacts the physician group and asks whether it has a separate assistance policy. Jordan does not assume the hospital application covers both bills, does not claim a fixed discount, and does not stop tracking the hospital's stated deadline. The outcome remains unknown until each organization responds.

## Follow up on an incomplete, approved, or denied application

If the hospital says the application is incomplete, ask for a written list of exactly what is missing and the deadline to provide it. Respond through the verified channel and retain proof.

If approved, request the determination, covered dates and accounts, eligibility period, calculation, and revised balance in writing. Check whether the assistance was applied to every account the approval actually covers.

If denied, ask for the specific policy criterion and any reconsideration process. A denial may reflect missing records, an excluded provider, a service outside the policy, or an eligibility decision. The correct response depends on the stated reason.

If the person does not qualify, ask about a payment plan or a provider-specific reduction without assuming one is required. [How Do You Negotiate a Hospital Bill in 2026?](/guides/how-to-negotiate-a-hospital-bill) offers a documented approach without promising a discount.
