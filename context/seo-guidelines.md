# medicalbillreader.com - SEO, AEO, and GEO Guidelines

## Content focus

Build a small, authoritative cluster around reading U.S. medical bills and EOBs, understanding billing-system terminology, preparing questions, and finding official appeal or assistance resources. Each page must answer one real visitor need better than a generic summary.

## Publication guardrails

- Research demand and existing results before creating a page; do not infer opportunity from a keyword pattern alone.
- Exact-code pages require a current primary source, manual YMYL review, clear code-set scope, and a licensing check. Do not mass-generate CPT or other code pages.
- Do not reproduce unlicensed descriptors, publish unsupported price benchmarks, or create doorway, duplicate, or thin pages.
- State what a code or field may represent on a claim; never infer what happened, what is correct, or what is owed from the code alone.
- Add meaningful internal links only when they help the reader continue the same task.

## Metadata

Use a concise page-specific title such as `[Topic] | Medical Bill Reader`. Descriptions must match the visible content and avoid "free," "accurate," savings, or error-detection promises unless the exact claim is currently true and qualified.

## Structured data

Use only schema types that match visible content and current search-engine eligibility. Markup does not make a page authoritative. FAQ markup must reflect visible questions and answers; Article or WebPage authorship must use truthful qualifications; dates must match the page. Validate output before release.

## AEO and GEO

- Lead with a direct, qualified answer in plain language; do not force an arbitrary word count.
- Separate document observations, general rules, exceptions, and next steps.
- Cite primary sources near time-sensitive or consequential claims and include a review date.
- Use descriptive headings, visible definitions, accessible HTML tables where useful, and concise FAQs only when they answer genuine follow-up questions.
- Make entity naming, author scope, methodology, corrections, and commercial relationships consistent across visible pages and machine-readable files.
