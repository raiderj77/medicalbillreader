# medicalbillreader.com - Style Guide

## Formatting and sourcing

- Use tables only when they make a source-backed comparison clearer. Include the source, jurisdiction or payer scope, and review date.
- Do not publish unsupported "typical cost" ranges or infer a fair, usual, allowed, or owed amount from a code alone.
- Define billing terms on first use and preserve plan-specific qualifications.
- Use numbered steps for processes, while telling readers to follow the deadlines and instructions on their own notice.
- Link changing claims to a current primary source and show a visible review date.
- Do not reproduce AMA CPT descriptors or other licensed code-set text without confirmed permission. A manually reviewed page may discuss a code system or quote only what licensing and applicable law permit.

## Terminology

- "EOB" means Explanation of Benefits. An EOB is not a bill and its patient-responsibility field is a plan calculation that must be compared with the provider's bill and plan terms.
- "CPT" means Current Procedural Terminology. Describe CPT as a copyrighted AMA code set used to report many professional services and procedures; do not say it covers every service or determines a price.
- Define deductible, coinsurance, copay, allowed amount, balance billing, and coordination of benefits as plan- and circumstance-dependent concepts.
- Prefer "medical bill" or "billing statement" to "invoice."
- Say a code was "reported," "billed," or "shown" rather than declaring what was performed or diagnosed.

## Calls to action

- "Upload a redacted bill or EOB for a plain-language summary."
- "Compare the report with your original document and verify questions with your provider and health plan."

Do not promise error detection, savings, a correct amount owed, a successful dispute, or a free analysis when eligibility has not been verified.
